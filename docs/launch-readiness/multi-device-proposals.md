# Multi-device accounts — the three competing proposals

Kept because the decision document's scorecard refers to them, and because
the rejected options record *why* the chosen one was chosen. Each was
argued at its strongest from an assigned perspective.



## Proposal 1 — event-sourced

# ARCHITECTURE PROPOSAL — event-sourced lean

**Thesis.** The transactional tables in `retail.db` already *are* an event log; they are just mutable by accident and locally-keyed by accident. Make them immutable and globally-keyed and replication becomes a set-union with no conflict rule. Everything mutable (`inventory_balances`, totals, PO status) becomes a derived projection. Do **not** build an `events` table or a CQRS framework — the existing rows are the events. That is the honest limit of this lean, and it is also where its value is.

**1. Identity.** Extend `commercial_runtime/identity/`, do not rewrite it. `users` gains `id TEXT` (UUID), `role ∈ {admin,manager,cashier}`, `pin_hash`, `branch_id`, `deleted_at`, and the single `@mt_require_subsystem('retail')` cell (`retail_api.py:200+`) is replaced by permission codes copied from `owner/app/models/staff.py` (`retail.sell`, `retail.refund`, `retail.discount`, `retail.stock.adjust`, `retail.reports`, `retail.cash.approve`, `retail.employees`). Admin is created once (`onboarding_routes.py:88`, unchanged); employees via the *existing* `create_employee()` + `secure_links` invite (`:369-427`) — the work is a UI on both platforms, which today has zero callers. **Identity is the one thing I refuse to event-source**: you cannot derive "this account is disabled" from an eventually-consistent merged log without letting a fired cashier keep selling. Accounts are server-authoritative in Owner Postgres and replicate *down* to each `registry.db` as a read-only projection; that also fixes the headline problem, since `registry.db` is per-install today (`registry_db.py:29`) and the admin's desktop and phone rows are unrelated. Password = account credential (enrols a device, opens back office). 4-digit PIN = attribution switch on an already-authenticated terminal; a PIN alone never authorises void-of-closed-sale, cost edits, employee management, or variance approval. **USER ≠ DEVICE/slot**: the device axis is `device_registry.py` (fingerprint, licence slot, `is_admin_device`) and stays orthogonal — it is not the user axis, and `commercial_runtime/identity/`'s device-admin must not be conflated with `users.role='admin'`. One cashier on two terminals = two sessions, one `actor_user_id`, both terminals' events merge into one shift figure. Two cashiers on one terminal = PIN switch between sales; every event carries `actor_user_id` **and** `terminal_id`; the drawer is not re-bound.

**2. Shared vs local.** Shared (replicated): catalogue, `users`/roles, `branches`, and the immutable facts — `sales`+`sale_items`, `returns`+`return_items`, `inventory_movements`, `payments`, `cash_movements`, `cash_sessions` headers, PO receipt events. Device-local: `sync_outbox`, `sync_cursor`, `doc_sequences`, and `inventory_balances` — which is a **cache, deleted from the wire entirely**, recomputed locally by `stock_reconciliation.py` (`_DRIFT_SQL:88` is already exactly the projection). `quantity_reserved` (`schema.py:1404`) is dead at 0; drop it. A drawer session is *written* only by its own terminal but *read* everywhere, so the admin sees the phone's Z-report on the desktop.

**3. Row identity.** UUID TEXT PKs on every replicated table, because `owner/app/sync/routes.py:168` casts `entity_id` to `uuid.UUID` and 400s the whole batch otherwise. Follow the proven recipe verbatim — `_migrate_products_to_uuid`, `schema.py:528-660`: `PRAGMA foreign_keys=OFF`, explicit `BEGIN`, build `*_new`, `id_map`, `DROP`+`RENAME` (never rename the original away, `:470-473`), rebuild every FK child remapping through `id_map`. Document numbers get a device prefix (`SALE-<dev4>-000101`, the Shopify `#1-1005` pattern) and `UNIQUE(company_id, sale_number)`; a global sequential number is never allocated offline.

**4. Conflict rules, by class.**
- *Immutable facts* (sale, sale_item, return, return_item, inventory_movement, payment, cash_movement): **no rule needed** — distinct UUIDs, `INSERT OR IGNORE`, set-union. Corrections are compensating events (`void_sale`, `adjustment`), never UPDATE/DELETE. `sales.status` stops being mutated.
- *Catalogue* (category/product/customer/supplier): **server-authoritative optimistic concurrency**, the only rule with industry precedent — add `row_version INTEGER`, Owner rejects stale with `VERSION_MISMATCH`, client refetches and re-applies. This also closes the full-row-snapshot clobber: push a changed-field mask, not the whole row. Not LWW.
- *Deletes*: **tombstones only** — `deleted_at`/`deleted_by`, physical DELETE forbidden. A tombstone carries the higher `row_version`, so a concurrent update cannot resurrect it.
- *PO / reorder*: receipt becomes an append-only `po_received(line, qty)` event; `status` and `received_qty` are derived sums. Two devices receiving one delivery then produces sum > ordered → a visible exception, not today's silent doubled `purchase_in` (`retail_api.py:1215-1230`). `reorder_requests.status` stops being a blind LWW upsert (`sync_service.py:414-421`).
- *Cash sessions*: re-key from branch to **terminal** — drop `idx_cash_sessions_one_open_per_branch` (`schema.py:1213`), add `UNIQUE(company_id, terminal_id) WHERE status='open'`. Each terminal is its own aggregate, so the conflict disappears by construction rather than by rule. Keep the ENDED-vs-CLOSED split you already earned in AUDIT-032.

**5. Offline.** *Can*: sell (cash + manual tender), take stock movements, open/close its own drawer, add a customer, return **against a sale present locally**. *Cannot*: create or edit products/prices, manage employees, approve a variance, refund a sale it has never seen, receive a PO. *Told*: a persistent banner — "Offline. Stock shown is last synced HH:MM, not live" (Loyverse's honest degradation beats a confidently wrong number, which is the original complaint); a queued-event count; logout and app-close blocked while the outbox is non-empty (Shopify loses offline orders exactly here); soft warning at 24h, hard stop-selling at 72h (Square). **Both devices sell the last unit offline**: both sales succeed. Each appends its own UUID `sale_out` row; on merge the ledger sums to −1 with no conflict resolution at all, `compute_drift` (`stock_reconciliation.py:99`) surfaces it, and it is attributable to two devices, two cashiers, two timestamps. It lands in an "oversold" exception queue for the admin — a business exception (backorder/substitute/refund), not corruption. No product on the market prevents this offline and I will not build distributed locking to try.

**6. Migration** (from `RETAIL_SCHEMA_VERSION = 12`, `schema.py:190`; each step ships alone).
- **v13 — additive, zero-rebuild**: add `uuid TEXT`, `row_version`, `deleted_at`, `actor_user_id`, `terminal_id`, `created_at_utc` to the transactional tables; backfill `uuid4()` per row; unique indexes. No table rebuild, no FK risk.
- **v14 — ledger seeding, the no-loss step**: for every `inventory_balances` row whose `(product_id, branch_id)` has no movements — every pre-existing install, plus `_seed_retail` (`schema.py:1726`) — insert one `opening_count` movement equal to current `quantity_on_hand`. Run `compute_drift` before and after; **refuse to advance `user_version` if drift ≠ 0**. After this the cache is provably derivable and the ledger is the truth in fact, not just in docstring.
- **v15 — UUID PKs** via the `:528-660` recipe, `id_map` read from v13's `uuid` column so it is deterministic and re-runnable. Order: `branches` → `inventory_movements`/`balances` → `sales`/`sale_items` → `returns`/`return_items` → `purchase_orders`/items → `payments`.
- **v16 — branch identity**: `branches` becomes a synced entity; at pairing the admin names each device's local branch, writing a `branch_alias` map so device B's local branch 1 folds into the company branch UUID. Without this the merged ledger sums across unrelated keys.
- **v17 — drawer**: backfill `cash_sessions.terminal_id` from this device's fingerprint, swap the partial unique index, force-close any session crossing a business date as an unverified variance.
- `registry.db` gets its own parallel migration (`users.id` UUID, `pin_hash`, permission codes). The phone's existing admin row is reconciled to the desktop's by (Owner-authenticated email, company) at first login; its pre-existing sales keep their own device id and push as historical events.

**7. Build order.** **P0** (v13+v14, nothing new syncs): attribution columns populated, ledger seeded, an admin "stock accuracy" screen exposing `compute_drift`. The product is unchanged but the complaint becomes measurable. **P1**: identity — surface the dormant `create_employee`/`get_employees` in both UIs, enforce `session_version` (the bumps at `onboarding_routes.py:437,462,490` currently revoke nothing), fix the fail-open at `mt_auth.py:226-227`, wire the dormant `user_device_is_authorized` (`device_registry.py:297`), permission codes, PIN switch. **P2** (v15-v17): UUIDs, branch identity, terminal-bound drawer — wire-ready, still not syncing. **P3**: replicate the append-only classes — extend the two literal tuples at `sync_service.py:292,326` and add apply branches; balances recomputed after each applied batch *inside the caller's* `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` (today's reconciler manages no transaction, `:153`). This is the phase where the admin finally sees one stock figure and one takings figure on both devices. **P4**: catalogue `row_version` + tombstones + field-level push; `users` as a synced read-only projection. **P5**: PO/reorder as events, oversell exception queue, Owner-side pruning of `owner_sync_events` below the slowest cursor.

**8. Risks / refusals.** *Refuse*: distributed stock reservations; global sequential document numbers offline; any till operation that blocks on the network; touching the `pg_advisory_xact_lock` critical section in `owner/app/sync/routes.py`; event-sourcing identity or licence state; a greenfield event-store rewrite of `retail.db`. *Risks*: ~5k events/day/device against an `owner_sync_events` table with no TTL and full-row JSONB — pruning is a P5 blocker, not a nicety. All-or-nothing apply means one poison event jams a licence permanently; add quarantine before P3. Clock skew silently corrupts every report predicate, since `metrics.py:498,510` bucket on a local-wall-clock string — order by server seq, report on `created_at_utc` + business-date offset, record observed skew at sync. And `repair_drift` will bake a duplicated movement in as truth, which UUID PKs + `INSERT OR IGNORE` prevent and nothing else does. Long-run, ledger recompute cost needs Square's `PHYSICAL_COUNT` checkpoint as a truncation point — a stocktake is an event, never an UPDATE.

## Proposal 2 — minimum-viable-change

Verified against the tree: `RETAIL_SCHEMA_VERSION = 12` with a single append-only `_migrate_retail_schema` (schema.py:861-910), `REGISTRY_SCHEMA_VERSION = 2` with the same convention (registry_db.py:40-48), allowlist tuple duplicated at sync_service.py:292 and :326, `_queue_sync_event` at retail_api.py:85.

# LEAN PROPOSAL — one admin account, two terminals, minimum blast radius

**Thesis.** Three levers do 90% of the work: (a) an additive `uid TEXT UNIQUE` column instead of a UUID PK rebuild; (b) widening the sync allowlist to the *append-only* tables only; (c) making catalogue/PO/user writes **admin-device-only**, which deletes the entire concurrent-update conflict class by policy instead of by algorithm. Owner and the sync transport are not touched at all.

**1. Identity.** Extend `registry.db`, do not rewrite. `users.role` widens from {admin, employee} to {admin, manager, cashier}; capabilities live in one new constant map `commercial_runtime/identity/capabilities.py` (`sale.void`, `sale.discount`, `stock.adjust`, `report.view`, `cash.close`, `user.manage`), enforced by a `@requires(cap)` decorator alongside the existing `@mt_require_subsystem('retail')`. No role table, no permission grid — `user_permissions` stays as-is. Add `users.uid TEXT` (wire identity), `pin_hash`, `updated_at`, `deleted_at`. **USER ≠ DEVICE**: the device is the licence slot (`devices`, `device_registry.py:50-66`, `is_admin_device`); the user is an account row. A terminal is *enrolled once* by the admin (email+password, one Flask session per install — unchanged), then the **acting user is PIN-switched** on top of that session and re-asserted per POS transaction. Cashier on two terminals: two independent sessions, no lock, no conflict — attribution is per row, not per session. Two cashiers on one terminal: PIN switch between transactions; both are attributed correctly without any multi-session Flask work. A PIN never authorises `user.manage`, cost/price edits, or a void of a closed sale — those re-prompt for password. Accounts sync as entity type `user` over the existing relay; because apply runs in a retail.db txn, user events land in a small `identity_inbox` table inside that batch and are drained into registry.db post-commit, idempotent by `uid`.

**2. Shared vs device-local.** Shared: `branches`, `users`, `sales`, `sale_items`, `returns`, `return_items`, `inventory_movements`, plus today's five catalogue types. Device-local, deliberately: `inventory_balances` (derived cache — syncing it creates a second truth; recomputed from the ledger by `stock_reconciliation.repair_drift` after every applied batch), `cash_sessions`/`cash_movements` (one physical drawer), `sync_outbox`/`sync_cursor`, `doc_sequences`. **Deferred, not local-forever: `purchase_orders`.** The PO *document* stays local; the `purchase_in` movement it emits syncs, so stock is correct on both devices — only PO status is invisible to the phone. Receiving and reorder-accept are gated to the admin device (`_is_admin_device()`, retail_api.py:169), which also kills the accept-on-both-devices double-PO bug (reorder_hook.py:117-120).

**3. Row identity — additive `uid`, not a PK rebuild.** Every synced transactional table gains `uid TEXT UNIQUE`, backfilled with a Python-generated UUID4 per existing row. Local `INTEGER AUTOINCREMENT` PKs and every existing FK stay exactly as they are; `uid` is the *wire* key, satisfying Owner's `uuid.UUID(entity_id)` gate (routes.py:168). Children carry the parent's uid on the wire (`sale_uid`, `branch_uid`, `product_uid`) and resolve to the receiver's local integer id on apply — so `inventory_balances UNIQUE(company_id, product_id, branch_id)` keeps working with local branch ids once `branches.uid` maps them. Document numbers keep the per-device prefix pattern: `_next_ref` (retail_api.py:2956) gains a device-fingerprint suffix so `SALE-000101-<cid>` can never collide.

**4. Conflict rules, per class.**
- *Append-only ledger* (`sale`, `sale_item`, `return`, `return_item`, `inventory_movement`, `cash_movement`): **no rule needed.** `INSERT … ON CONFLICT(uid) DO NOTHING`. Replay-safe by construction, which also fixes the double-ledger-row hazard `stock_reconciliation` currently has.
- *Sale/return status*: not an UPDATE — a `sale.void` event. The transition is **monotonic** (completed→voided, never back), so re-application is a no-op. Not LWW.
- *Catalogue* (category/product/customer/supplier): writes are **admin-device-only**, so it is single-writer and concurrent update cannot occur. Belt-and-braces: add `row_version INTEGER` and apply only when incoming > local; drop and log otherwise. Residual (two different fields edited concurrently) is deferred and *only* safe because of the single-writer gate — the day the owner wants product edits from the phone, this rule comes due immediately.
- *Users*: single-writer (admin device), LWW on `updated_at`.
- *Deletes*: **no hard DELETE on any synced entity.** `deleted_at TIMESTAMP` on catalogue + users; a delete is an update event; readers filter. This is the anti-resurrection answer and it cannot slip past Phase 4.
- *Balances*: not merged — recomputed. *Drawer sessions*: not merged — see below.

**5. Offline.** Allowed: sell, cash in/out, open/close drawer, return **against a sale present locally**. Blocked with a plain message: create/edit product, customer, supplier, user; receive a PO; refund a sale this device has never seen. Told: a persistent banner "Offline — stock last synced 14:32"; following Loyverse, once the last successful sync is older than 30 min the POS tile **hides the on-hand number** rather than show a confident wrong one — showing a wrong figure is what produced the original "stock is not accurate" complaint. Hard stop at 72h offline: new sales blocked until reconnect. Logout and app-exit are blocked while `sync_outbox` is non-empty (Shopify's documented offline-order-loss mode). **Both devices sell the last unit:** both sales succeed. Each appends its own `sale_out` movement with a distinct `uid`; on merge both rows survive, the recomputed sum is −1, `compute_drift` (stock_reconciliation.py:99) surfaces it as an *oversold* exception attributed to device and timestamp. We do not prevent it, we do not build reservations, and the UI says so.

**6. Migration.** `RETAIL_SCHEMA_VERSION` 12→13; one new idempotent step `_migrate_add_multi_terminal(conn)` appended **last** in `_migrate_retail_schema` (schema.py:876-910), exactly the convention that file documents. It is `ALTER TABLE ADD COLUMN` + backfill + `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX` only — **no table rebuild, no `id_map`, no FK remap, no DROP/RENAME**, so `_migrate_products_to_uuid`'s risk profile is not repeated on live sales data. Adds: `uid` to sales/sale_items/returns/return_items/inventory_movements/branches; `user_uid` to sales/returns/inventory_movements/cash_sessions (nullable — existing free-text `cashier`/`created_by` values are kept, nothing is lost); `deleted_at`+`row_version` to the four catalogue tables; `cash_sessions.terminal_id` backfilled with this device's fingerprint, then `idx_cash_sessions_one_open_per_branch` is dropped and recreated as `(company_id, terminal_id) WHERE status='open'`. `REGISTRY_SCHEMA_VERSION` 2→3 adds users' `uid`/`pin_hash`/`updated_at`/`deleted_at`. **Existing installs:** single-device installs (nearly all of them) migrate with zero loss and no user action — the backfilled history simply pushes once on first sync, bounded by the existing 200-event chunking. The rare install already running two independently diverged devices must nominate a primary; the secondary exports a CSV backup, then clears its transactional tables and pulls the primary's history. There is no honest way to auto-merge two histories that were never coordinated, and pretending otherwise would double the ledger.

**7. Build order — five phases, each shippable, each leaving the product working.**
1. **Identity surface, no sync.** registry v3; role/capability map; expose the already-written `/api/admin/employees` routes in `app-shell.js` and Compose; PIN switch. Also fix, unconditionally: `mt_login_required` (mt_auth.py:226-227) fails open on DB exception and never checks `session_version`, so the bumps at onboarding_routes.py:437/462/490 revoke nothing — that is a security bug, not a scope question. Wire the dormant `user_device_is_authorized`.
2. **Attribution.** retail v13 columns; stamp `user_uid` on sales/returns/movements/drawer; "by employee" in reports. Still single-device — and already answers "who voided this".
3. **Terminal-bound drawer.** `terminal_id` + index swap. Fixes the phone's takings folding into the desktop Z report (`_cash_session_report`, retail_api.py:2339) even before sync exists.
4. **Ledger sync — this is the phase that delivers "same data".** Widen the allowlist tuple in *both* places (sync_service.py:292 and :326) to `branch, user, sale, sale_item, return, return_item, inventory_movement`; insert-or-ignore by `uid`; tombstones + `row_version` on catalogue; run `repair_drift` under its own `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` after each applied batch. Owner unchanged.
5. **Hardening.** Offline UX (banner, stale-stock hiding, 72h stop, logout block); Owner-side pruning of `owner_sync_events` below the slowest device cursor; record clock skew at sync.

**8. Risks and refusals.** I would **refuse**: to touch `pg_advisory_xact_lock` or anything else in the transport; to rebuild sales/returns/movements onto UUID primary keys on live pre-launch data; to promote Owner into the merchant identity authority in this pass; to sync `inventory_balances`; to build distributed stock reservations; to ship Phase 4 without Owner-side retention pruning (≈5k events/day/licence, full-row JSONB, no TTL, no DELETE anywhere in `owner/app/sync/routes.py`); to sync `purchase_orders` now. **Named costs I am accepting:** two id spaces (local int PK + wire `uid`) is a permanent smell and a future clean-up costs the rebuild I just deferred — every new join must know which space it is in. Report predicates still sit on local-clock `created_at` (metrics.py:498,510), so Phase 4 makes clock skew *visible* rather than fixing it; ledger ordering moves to server seq, but daily buckets stay local-time until a later pass. And the single-writer gate is a policy, not a property: it holds only as long as catalogue and PO editing stay on the admin device.

## Proposal 3 — server-authoritative

ARCHITECTURE PROPOSAL — SERVER-AUTHORITATIVE. Root: `C:\Users\MSI\Desktop\aura-fullsuits\.claude\worktrees\ci-hardening-w0.3-continue\`; paths below are relative to it.

**Thesis.** Owner CC is already the licensing authority and the relay holding a per-license, seq-ordered, replay-proof log. Make it the authority for *identity* and for *arbitration of mutable master data*. Do **not** make it the write path for selling. Local sqlite stays a durable write-ahead cache, not a cache you can lose.

**1. Identity.** New Postgres tables in `owner/app/models/merchant_identity.py` — `merchant_companies(id uuid, license_id)`, `merchant_users(id uuid, company_id, email, password_hash, role_id, pin_hash, status, session_version, row_version)`, `merchant_roles`/`merchant_role_permissions` seeded OWNER/MANAGER/CASHIER, `merchant_terminals(id uuid, company_id, branch_id, device_fingerprint, revoked_at)`. Model on `owner/app/models/staff.py` (invitations, session-version revocation, login attempts) — copy the shape, do **not** reuse `owner_staff_*`: that models Aura's own back-office and conflating tenants is a security defect. `commercial_runtime/identity/registry_db.py` keeps its `users` table but it becomes a **replica**: add `remote_user_id`, `row_version`, `password_hash` (cached verifier), `pin_hash`, `deleted_at_utc`. Owner mints ids; devices never insert a user locally. `company_id` stops being `md5(admin_email)` (`onboarding_routes.py:138`) and becomes Owner-issued, bound to the license.
**Auth.** Keep `auth_routes.py:24-55` and the Flask signed-cookie session — but the credential is verified against the *replicated* hash, so login works with no network. Fix two real bugs first: `mt_auth.py:226-227` fails open on DB error (close it), and `session_version` is never checked (`mt_auth.py:203-230`) so the bumps at `onboarding_routes.py:437,462,490` revoke nothing. With replication, session_version becomes the revocation channel: Owner bumps, the bump syncs, the device kills the session on next tick.
**User ≠ device.** A licence slot binds a *terminal* to the company+branch (activate `device_registry.grant_user_device`/`user_device_is_authorized:273,297` — currently zero production callers). A user is a person. **One cashier, two terminals:** normal — she logs in on both; both sessions are hers; every row carries `user_id` *and* `terminal_id`. **Two cashiers, one terminal:** terminal stays enrolled and logged in; a 4-digit PIN switches the acting user per transaction (Square's personal-passcode model). PIN is attribution, never authorization — voids, price/cost edits, employee management, variance approval require the password.
**Employee UI.** `/api/admin/employees` already exists and is admin-gated but nothing calls it. Wire `products/retail/frontend/` and an Android screen. Replace the single `@mt_require_subsystem('retail')` literal on ~80 routes (`retail_api.py:200+`) with capability codes — `retail.sell`, `retail.refund`, `retail.discount`, `retail.stock.adjust`, `retail.reports`, `retail.cash.close`, `retail.cash.approve`, `retail.admin`. The existing `user_permissions` table takes them unchanged.

**2. Shared vs local.** Shared (relayed): users/roles/permissions, terminals, **branches**, category, product, customer, supplier, reorder_request, `sales`+`sale_items`, `returns`+`return_items`, `payments`, `inventory_movements`, `purchase_orders`+items, `cash_sessions`+`cash_movements`. Device-local (never relayed): `sync_outbox`, `sync_cursor`, `doc_sequences`, `held_sales`, `inventory_balances`. Justification: the drawer is a *physical* object so a session is terminal-**owned** (only its terminal may write it) — but ownership ≠ invisibility, so it still replicates read-only, otherwise the admin's phone can't show today's takings. `inventory_balances` is pure derivation (`stock_reconciliation.py:88` is `SUM(quantity) GROUP BY product_id, branch_id`); syncing a cache invites two truths. Recompute locally after every applied batch. `quantity_reserved` (`schema.py:1404`) is dead — drop it.
**Cash fix:** `cash_sessions` is keyed to a branch with `idx_cash_sessions_one_open_per_branch` (`schema.py:1213-1215`), so two devices in one shop share a drawer and `_cash_session_report` folds phone takings into the desktop Z. Add `terminal_id`, index unique per (company, terminal). Adopt OPENED→ENDED→CLOSED: counting and approving are separate acts — this is the AUDIT-032 property you already shipped.

**3. Row identity.** All synced tables move to `id TEXT PRIMARY KEY` = UUIDv7 (time-ordered, index-friendly). Follow the proven `_migrate_products_to_uuid` pattern (`schema.py:528-660`): `foreign_keys=OFF`, explicit BEGIN, build `_new`, `id_map`, DROP+RENAME (never rename the original away), rebuild FK children from the `referencing` list. Owner's relay already requires this — `owner/app/sync/routes.py:168` UUID-parses `entity_id` and `models/sync.py:33` is a Postgres `UUID` column, which is exactly why POs are excluded today. Document numbers keep a **device prefix**: `_next_ref` (`retail_api.py:2956`) becomes `SALE-<terminal_short>-000101`, per-device monotonic; never allocate a global sequential number offline. `branches.id` must become a UUID too, or the `UNIQUE(company_id,product_id,branch_id)` balance key stays non-portable.

**4. Conflict rules — three policies, not one.**
- *Append-only, no rule needed:* `inventory_movements`, `sale_items`, `return_items`, `payments`, `cash_movements`, and the `sales`/`returns` headers. Distinct UUIDs, idempotent apply, order by Owner's seq. Merge is arithmetic.
- *Server-authoritative optimistic concurrency:* product, category, customer, supplier, branch, users/roles, `purchase_orders`, `cash_sessions`, `reorder_request`. Add `row_version INTEGER` + `updated_at_utc`. Owner compares versions inside the existing `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext(license_id))` section and rejects stale with `VERSION_MISMATCH`; client refetches and re-applies. Also switch update events from full-row snapshot to a **changed-fields delta** — that alone kills the one-field-edit-clobbers-everything gap. A rejected offline edit lands in a local `sync_conflicts` table and is shown, never silently dropped. Explicit LWW nowhere. `sales.status` (void) is the one exception: first-void-wins, later voids are no-ops.
- *Deletes:* soft only. `deleted_at_utc` + `row_version` bump, replicated as an ordinary versioned update, so a concurrent update with a lower version loses and cannot resurrect. Purge tombstones only below the slowest device's cursor. Same job prunes `owner_sync_events`, which today has no TTL and no DELETE anywhere — mandatory once volume goes from tens to ~5k events/day/device.
- *Ordering:* order by Owner seq, keep device local time for display, record observed clock skew at sync. `metrics.py` currently filters on `date(created_at)` of a **local wall clock** (`:498,:510`) — add `created_at_utc` and move every report predicate onto it.

**5. Offline.** Allowed: sell (cash and manual tender), hold/recall, cash in/out, open/close a drawer, refund **only** against a sale present locally, view catalogue and last-synced stock. Blocked: create/edit product, category, customer or supplier; create or disable an employee; change a role; approve a cash variance; receive a PO; stocktake. This is Shopify/Lightspeed's line and it is the right one — those all need authoritative state. Told to the user: a persistent banner "Offline since 14:20 — 412 unsynced", stock shown as "as of 14:20" not as a confident number (Loyverse hides it rather than lie), and Square's 24h soft / 72h hard expiry — past 72h the terminal refuses new sales and says why. Block logout and app exit while the outbox is non-empty (Shopify loses offline orders exactly this way).
**Two devices sell the last unit offline:** both sales complete. No reservation, no distributed lock. Each appends its own UUID `sale_out` movement; on merge the ledger sums to −1, `compute_drift` (`stock_reconciliation.py:99`) surfaces it attributed to terminal and time, and it lands in an exception queue for the admin. A refused sale is worse than an oversell. Today this same case produces silent permanent divergence — which is the original "stock is not accurate" complaint.

Never guess. v16: drop `quantity_reserved`, swap the cash-session index. Registry: bump its own schema, add `remote_user_id`/`row_version`/`pin_hash`.
**Adoption.** First upgraded device runs `claim`: its existing admin row is matched to Owner by email + license proof, employees are uploaded, its history is pushed as the license baseline. Every subsequent device runs `adopt`: local rows get fresh UUIDs and push as events keyed `(terminal_id, legacy_local_id)` so a retry cannot double-apply. Both devices' real histories merge — the ledger sums, balances recompute, nothing is discarded. Ship `adopt` behind a dry-run that reports counts and drift before committing.

**8. Risks / refusals.** Refuse: distributed stock locks or online-only reservations; online-only login; routing sale creation through Owner; touching `pg_advisory_xact_lock` in `owner/app/sync/routes.py`; last-write-wins on money or stock; hard deletes on any synced table; auto-merging conflicting *user* edits — a person's role is a security decision, so it is server-authoritative and offline changes are simply refused. Biggest risks: the UUID remaps are destructive and need a verified backup plus a dry-run per install; the free-text→`user_id` backfill will leave historical rows unattributed and pretending otherwise would be fabrication; ~5k events/day/license makes retention a launch blocker, not a follow-up; and merging two divergent histories at adoption is a one-shot operation that must be idempotent and reversible before it runs on a real shop.
**Where my lean is wrong:** authentication and selling must be offline-capable, so Owner cannot be the runtime authority for either — it is the authority for *issuance* and *arbitration*, and the device is the authority for *capture*.
